# Proposals

The **contribution quarantine**. It is the only way new memory enters the graph: what a contributor (or an agent with a contributor token) sends **is not incorporated until someone approves it**. That way an agent that makes a mistake, or a doubtful text, does not pollute the memory.

## Flow
```
Submitted ──► (optional decider) ──► Pending ──► Approved ──► Ingested
                                        │              │
                                        └► Rejected    └► Failed ──► (retry, or reject)
```
1. **Submit.** A contributor ends up *Pending*. A curator (or higher) ends up *Approved* directly ("approved on submission"), unless the secret detector flagged something (see below).
2. **Decider (optional).** If a decider model is configured, it evaluates the proposal against the **brain's scope**: its description and the entity and relation types of its [[Ontology]]. Without either, it only checks that the proposal is factual. It answers two questions (is it factual? what action does it recommend?) and, if both exceed the **confidence threshold**, it approves the proposal by itself. If not, it leaves it for a curator. **It never rejects on its own**: what does not fit goes to a curator.
3. **Review.** A curator opens the proposal and **approves** or **rejects** it. **+ Note** (or <kbd>N</kbd>) opens an optional note, and **What deciding does** unfolds what each button will do; what must be read before approving (a correction invalidates a fact, flagged secrets get masked) is always shown. After deciding, a notice says what happened and offers **Undo**. In **To review** it appears just above the note and the buttons of the next proposal, on one line, so nothing on the screen moves; elsewhere it appears above the list. Once there is nothing left to do with it (the decision was taken back, or the proposal is in the graph) it goes away after a few seconds, and it never links to the proposal that is already open. A screen reader announces each step once (decided, entering the graph, the result), not the countdown, and says how to take a decision back. The open proposal's title is a heading that names the detail, and each queue entry is read by its title, with its preview and markers as its description. After a decision the focus moves to the title of the next proposal, so a button that stays where it was is not pressed again by accident; after an undo, the proposal opens again and the focus goes back to its entry in the queue (on a narrow screen, to its title, with the notice under it). Moving through the queue shows the next proposal at once, while its full text loads:
   - **An approval waits 10 seconds** before ingestion, and can be undone in that time: the proposal goes back to *Pending* and nothing reaches the graph. Afterwards the notice follows the ingestion until it ends and says how many entities and facts were created (or that none was extracted). What is already in the graph is corrected by invalidating its facts, like the rest of the memory. Approvals by the decider and by webhooks do not wait.
   - **A rejection can be undone or reopened** at any time, unless its text was purged by the retention period.
4. **Ingestion.** An approved proposal is sent to the LLM, which extracts entities and facts and adds them to the graph (invalidating those it contradicts). It becomes *Ingested*. If it fails, it stays *Failed* and can be **retried**, or **rejected** when it is not worth trying again (a retry still waiting is cancelled; one already running is left to finish).

> The decider knows what the brain is about from its **description**: write one when creating or editing the brain (Administration → Brains, see [[Brains and members]]).

## The screen
It has three tabs:
- **To review** (curators; it opens by default) is the queue of what waits for a decision.
- **Decided** (curators) is the history: approved, ingested, rejected and failed ones, in a table with filters.
- **My proposals** has the form to propose and what you have sent, in every state.

A **contributor** only has the last one. The tab is kept in the address (`?tab=decididas`), so a reload or a shared link opens the same one; an older link with `?estado=decided` opens **Decided**.

### To review
On the left, the **queue** of pending proposals; on the right, the open proposal with its detail and the **Reject** and **Approve** buttons always in view. On a narrow screen you see one at a time: over the proposal, **Back to the queue**, where it is in the queue («3 of 16») and **Previous** / **Next** to move on without going back; and a proposal you open starts at its title, which takes the focus, wherever the queue was scrolled; **Back to the queue** puts the focus back on its entry. On a phone the decision stays fixed at the bottom of the screen, within reach, with **Reject** and **Approve** 44px tall on touch screens, sharing one row equally with **Approve** on the right (there the rejection's reason is in its name and its note, not on the button), where the shortcut hint is hidden. The queue only shows the pager when it does not fit one page. In the queue, a long marker sends the author and age to the next line instead of hiding them, and on touch screens each checkbox answers within 44px around it and the selection bar's buttons are 44px tall.

- **Ordered by risk and age:** corrections first, then the ones with a secret to review, then the ones the brain already knows (marked **Possible duplicate** when a current fact says nearly the same, or **Possibly no longer true** when they resemble a fact that stopped being true (the same words as the fact and the rejection); a possible duplicate, what is possibly no longer true and a secret to review are marked in amber (a correction has its own filled marker), while a low confidence gets a neutral marker with an icon), then those with a low declared confidence (below 0.6) and, inside each group, the oldest first. Each entry says who sent it and how long it has been waiting.
- **Keyboard shortcuts:** <kbd>J</kbd>/<kbd>K</kbd> go to the next or the previous one (a screen reader hears which one and where, «#12 · title (3 of 16)»), <kbd>A</kbd> approves, <kbd>R</kbd> rejects, <kbd>X</kbd> marks for a batch decision the entry with the focus (or the open one), <kbd>N</kbd> opens the note (inside it, <kbd>Esc</kbd> goes back to the proposal and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> approves, as <kbd>A</kbd> does) and <kbd>Z</kbd> undoes the last decision while the notice still offers it. They act only while the focus is in the queue or the open proposal (a click anywhere on them is enough), so a key typed or dictated elsewhere on the page never decides anything, and they do nothing while you type in the note. The queue is a single <kbd>Tab</kbd> stop: <kbd>↑</kbd>/<kbd>↓</kbd>, <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> open the entry they reach, and <kbd>J</kbd>/<kbd>K</kbd> take the focus with them when it was in the queue, so the entry with the focus and the open proposal are always the same one. Its first stop, shown when the keyboard reaches it, is **Go to the open proposal**, which moves the focus to the proposal's title. After a decision the next one opens. On a narrow screen <kbd>A</kbd> and <kbd>R</kbd> act only while the proposal is open, never on one you have not seen. On a proposal with a secret to review, <kbd>A</kbd> opens the **Mask and approve** confirmation. In a confirmation that does not delete anything, <kbd>Enter</kbd> presses its main button; one opened with <kbd>A</kbd> gives the focus back to the proposal's title when it closes.
- **In batches:** check several (or **Select all**) and use **Approve** or **Reject** on the queue's bar, which keeps its height with or without a selection and says how many are selected and how many of them are decided apart (on a phone, only how many); **Clear the selection** (✕) empties it. A confirmation, with its buttons always in view, lists them by number and title (folded behind **Show which are approved** when there are more than three), says how many of the selected ones it decides («Approve 2 of 3?») and takes a common note. Corrections, proposals with a secret to review and the ones the brain already knows, has very close or has not checked yet **are not approved in a batch**: they stay selected, to decide one at a time. Only what you see in the queue goes into the batch.
- In the queue, the open proposal shows its kind only when it is a correction: every proposal there is pending, and adding is the usual kind. Beside its text, the **Proposal details** sheet says who sent it, when, from where and the author's confidence («not given» when there is none), and what the decider made of it: its verdict first («Referred to a curator»), why in one sentence, then its two answers with their confidence, its model and its threshold. In a narrow panel the sheet comes after the text. The text and what the brain knows stop at about 72 characters a line. Dates and numbers follow the interface language (in Spanish, 0,6 and 25/3/26). The **Kind** selector (*All*, *Add*, *Correct*) filters the queue and the tables, and its numbers count inside the state on screen (the decided ones, the rejected ones…); it only shows when there are both kinds. The **To review** tab shows how many are pending only while another tab is open. Under the buttons, a line recalls the **Shortcuts** (they act with the focus in the queue or the proposal).

When corrections are waiting, a notice says how many, because each one invalidates a fact and only a person decides it. **Show only the pending corrections** filters the queue.

### The tables
**Decided** and **My proposals** show **#** (the proposal number), **Kind**, **Title**, **Author**, **Confidence**, **State**, **Submitted** and **Decided**: who decided it (a curator, the decider, a webhook, or its author on submission) and when. The kind is a marker with an icon and a word, **Adds** or **Corrects**, and a correction's marker is filled, so it stands out without relying on colour; the **Kind** column only shows when the rows on screen are of both kinds. Authors are shown by name, here and in the queue, with their address in the tooltip. You can filter by title and by author (**All authors**), sort by title, author, confidence, submission or decision date (by default, the newest submitted first, as the **Submitted** column shows, by their date even when imported later), and move through the pages with the pager below. Filters and sort order apply to **every proposal**, not only to the rows on screen, so old proposals are always reachable.

> A **reader** neither sees proposals nor can create them. A **contributor** sees only their own; a **curator** or higher sees all.

## Creating a proposal
In **My proposals**, the form (it can be folded away), with:
- **Title:** short and descriptive ("Database migration to PostgreSQL 17").
- **Content:** *what happened or what needs remembering, with exact dates and names*.
- **Confidence (0–1, optional):** how much you trust that data. It is shown to the reviewer.

The button is **Propose** for a contributor and **Create and approve** for a curator: a curator's proposal skips the queue and enters the graph, as the form says, unless the secret detector flags something. Writing tips: [[Writing good memory]].

## Corrections
A **correction** is a proposal that does not add memory: it asks to **invalidate one fact** that is no longer true. Agents send it with the MCP tool `correct` or `POST /proposals/corrections`, pointing to the fact by its uuid (the one `facts_about`, `timeline` and `whats_new` give), with the reason and, optionally, the text that replaces it and the date it stopped being true.
- Its title starts with "Correction:" and its detail shows the **fact to invalidate** (subject → relation → target, since when it was current and its uuid), the **reason** and **what replaces it**.
- The decider **never** approves a correction, and over MCP it always waits for a person, even with a curator token. Through the REST API, a curator who sends one applies it at once.
- On approval the fact is **invalidated, never deleted**, as of the correction date; the step **Fact invalidated** shows it. If there is a replacement, it is ingested like any memory, with that date. The invalidation also goes to the vocabulary history, like those made from [[Facts]] or [[Contradictions]].
- If the fact stopped being current while the correction waited, approving it gives an error and the correction stays pending: reject it.
- **Reactivate the fact** in the correction puts the fact back as it was. An ingested replacement is left as it is.

### Reactivating a fact invalidated by mistake
A correction with `action: "reactivate"` asks the opposite: to make an **invalidated** fact current again, for example when a new fact superseded it wrongly. Its title starts with "Reactivation:", its marker says **Reactivates**, and its detail shows the **fact to reactivate** (and since when it is invalidated) with the reason. It is decided like any correction: by a person, one at a time. On approval the fact is current again (the step **Fact reactivated**), and the change is in the vocabulary history; **Undo the reactivation** invalidates it again as it was.

A memory whose text explains that a fact is still true does **not** reactivate it: its text is extracted into new facts and the old one stays invalidated. That is why a pending free-text proposal says **What approving it does**: its entities and relations are only extracted after it is approved, and it never invalidates or reactivates a fact reliably. To do that, use a correction, structured claims or **Reactivate** on the fact in [[Facts]].

## Structured claims
A proposal sent by an agent or over the API may carry **claims** next to its text: subject, relation and value, and optionally since when and which current fact it replaces. When it is sent, each one is compared with what the brain knows, and the detail shows them under **Claims** with their verdict:
- **Confirms a current fact:** approving it records a confirmation of that fact (it renews its shelf life) instead of a new fact. If every claim confirms, nothing is ingested.
- **Replaces facts:** approving it invalidates the facts listed, once the text is ingested, as of the claim's date. Like a correction, it is decided one at a time: the decider and a batch approval leave it to you. Each invalidation is in the vocabulary history and can be undone.
- **Looks like a replacement:** the subject has other current values in a relation that is not functional; they are left alone.
- **New:** nothing current in that relation.

Once applied, each claim says when. The agent guide (`docs/AGENTS.md`) explains how to send them.

## Retrievable after ingesting
A fact can be ingested well and never be found, because newer, more detailed facts bury it. After a proposal is ingested, the recall is asked one **control question** per new fact (up to 5), built from the subject and the relation («Vaultwarden runs on»). A proposal whose fact does not come back among the first 8 says **Ingested, but not retrievable** in its history, with the question and what came first. To fix it, rewrite the fact with the name people ask by, add an alias to the entity, or invalidate what buries it.

The questions are internal: no agent sees them. Each one is an embedding, and they are limited per brain and hour (*Retrievability checks per hour*; *Check that what is ingested can be retrieved* turns them off). [[Memory health]] counts the share of proposals that are retrievable.

## By category
**By category** groups what is pending by what produced it: the curator's contradictions, facts a verifier contradicts, corrections, possible duplicates, proposals close to something no longer true, and new ones. Each category shows how many are pending, a sample and its **precision**: the share of its proposals that people approved, over the last 50 they decided.

After looking at the sample, **Approve the category** or **Reject the category** decides all its pending proposals, up to 200. Proposals with a secret to review and corrections are left to decide one at a time. An approval waits the usual 10 seconds before ingestion.

**Automatic approval.** A brain administrator can tick **Approve this category automatically**, only once the category has earned it: at least 20 decisions by people and a precision of 95 % (adjustable through the API). From then on its proposals are approved within minutes, as «automatic» in the history and the audit log, and can be undone like any other. Automatic approvals never count towards the precision. If the precision of people's decisions falls below the bar, the category goes back to manual and the **autoapprove.disabled** notification says so. Nothing flagged by the secret scanner is ever approved this way. The email summary lists the curator's and verifiers' proposals by category, with a link here.

## Secrets in proposals
Before a proposal is stored (from the interface, the API or the `remember` tool over MCP), its title and content go through the **secret detector**: cloud and AI provider keys, tokens, private keys, JWTs, `Authorization` headers, passwords in URLs… Some findings are **certain**; a random-looking value assigned to `password`, `secret` or `token` is only **possible**. What happens depends on the brain's policy (Administration → Brain → **Secrets**):

| Policy | Certain secrets | Possible secrets |
|---|---|---|
| **Mask** (default) | Replaced with `[secret removed: type]` before storing | Flagged, left untouched |
| **Block** | The proposal is **rejected** with an error that names the type of secret | Flagged, left untouched |
| **Only warn** | Flagged, left untouched | Flagged, left untouched |

- **A flagged proposal always waits for a curator**, even when a curator sent it, and the decider does not approve it on its own.
- **It is never approved as written.** Its **Approve** button becomes **Mask and approve**. A confirmation names the types of secret; then every flagged value in the title and the text is replaced with `[secret removed: type]` and the proposal is approved. The original value is not kept, not even if you undo the approval. The audit log records which types were masked (`proposal.secrets_masked`), never the values. Over the API it is `POST /proposals/{id}/mask-and-approve`; `POST /proposals/{id}/approve` answers 409 for such a proposal.
- **With Mask, a curator's proposal is stored already masked and approved at once:** nothing is left for anyone to review, because the value never reached the memory.
- **The author is told when submitting:** the form says which types of secret were masked or flagged, the `POST /proposals` response carries the same text in `secrets_notice`, and the `remember` result adds it, so an agent knows its text was altered.
- Afterwards, the list marks those proposals (**Secret masked** or **Secret to review**, also the ones approved at once) and the detail shows **Secrets detected**: what was masked and what was flagged for review. The value itself is never shown, stored in the audit log or sent in notifications.
- Keep secrets in a password manager and store in memory only **where** they are.

## A proposal's detail
When you open it you see a **timeline**:
- **Submitted:** who and when, the declared confidence and the **source** (where the author says it comes from, and the format it arrived in).
- **What the brain already knows** (on pending proposals that add knowledge): the facts closest to the proposal's text, each in its own card as Ask shows a fact: its text, its two entities as chips coloured by type and linked to their pages, the relation in words between them («hosted on», the name as written in its tooltip), and on that line its similarity and since when it holds (or its dates when it is no longer true). The fact the proposal is read against comes first, headed **What the brain says**; the other close ones follow under **Other similar facts**. All this is to see whether it repeats or contradicts something. A first line says what to look at, as a warning when the brain already knows it (a possible duplicate, or something that stopped being true): **Possible duplicate** when the brain already says nearly the same, a warning when it resembles something that stopped being true, something very similar, or nothing nearly the same. For a possible duplicate, something close to a fact no longer true, or something very close to a current fact, that fact is shown as **What the brain says**, and when the proposal almost repeats it, its own text, at the top of the detail, underlines the words it adds to it, so the two read one against the other without repeating the proposal. A line right under the text says what the underline means and lists those words. When most of the proposal is new, nothing is underlined (almost every word would be) and its text reads clean; the verdict says it is mostly new. When its text adds no content word, that line says so, and when that fact is no longer true, it says the proposal repeats it. A mostly new proposal only shares its subject with the fact: the verdict says so («Mostly new: it shares its subject with something the brain already knows…»), the fact carries no **Very close**, and the queue does not mark it. A mostly new proposal is not taken for something that stopped being true just because it shares a few words with it. In the queue, a pending proposal shows its **Proposal details** sheet (see above) instead of a three-step timeline. Each fact is marked **Nearly the same** (its words, or nearly identical embeddings, and the proposal adds no word of content the fact lacks; words like «como», «para» or «mismo» do not count, an identifier such as pve-norte-02 is one word, a number counts however short it is, and words are compared without accents and by their stem, so «replica» and «replicar» are the same word) or **Very similar** (its words are in the proposal, which adds more; or a high similarity), the closest first, with its similarity shown; a fact that is no longer true is struck through and marked **No longer true**. Facts that are only related stay folded behind **N facts only related**, last, so they do not read as evidence; unfolded, a sentence says they are there for context. A contradiction cannot be told from the text alone: the closest facts are there to check it. When nothing is similar it says so: it would be new knowledge. The brain checks this in the background as soon as a proposal arrives (when there is an extraction engine), again whenever its detail is opened, and, for the pending ones never checked (they arrived without an engine, or the check failed), whenever a curator opens the queue, so the queue can rank and mark all of them. **Approve** is always the main button, in the same place. With a possible duplicate the rejection reads **Reject: already known**, and with something that resembles a fact no longer true **Reject: no longer true**: either leaves that reason as the note when you wrote none (<kbd>R</kbd> does the same). Approving either asks for a confirmation that quotes that fact, with the button and with <kbd>A</kbd>; the weight is on the safe choices: that rejection and **Cancel**, where the focus starts, so <kbd>Enter</kbd> never decides by reflex; **Approve anyway** is a quiet button apart, last when the buttons stack on a phone. Something only very close that adds little («Very close to something the brain already knows: check what the proposal adds») is not treated as known: it asks for no confirmation, but the queue marks it with a neutral **Very close** and it is not approved in a batch: it stays selected, to read what it adds and decide it apart. Until this check answers, **Approve** waits (it shows it is busy): <kbd>A</kbd> pressed meanwhile is kept, and approves, or asks first, once the brain has answered. This lookup does not count towards recall quality.
- **Review:** whether it was approved on submission, approved by a curator (with their note) or decided by the decider. If it was the decider, it shows which model, the threshold, its two answers with their confidence and the verdict (**Approved automatically** or **Referred to a curator**). If the decider was unavailable, the error is shown.
- **Ingestion into the graph:** the id of the created episode, the date, and a **report**: how many entities and facts were created, how many entities ended up *untyped*, which types fell outside the ontology, which entities were merged into their primary, which were not merged because the match was ambiguous, and how many relations were normalised by an alias.

## Curator actions
- **Approve / Reject** (with an optional note).
- **Reopen** a rejected one, or **Undo the approval** of an approved one whose ingestion has not started yet.
- **Ingest now** or **Retry ingestion** when an approved one has not been ingested or failed; a failed one can also be **rejected**.
- **Reactivate the fact** in an applied correction.

## Notices you may see
- **"No LLM engine configured":** approved proposals are not ingested and stay in "Approved". An administrator must configure it in [[AI providers]].
- **"No decider":** all contributors' proposals wait for a curator.
- **Ingestion failed:** see the reason in the detail and in [[Jobs]]. The most common errors are a provider rate limit or a model that does not return valid JSON ("reasoning" models sometimes need a proxy that disables *thinking*).

## From an agent
Agents create proposals through the REST API or the `remember` tool of the MCP server, and corrections with the `correct` tool: [[Connect agents]]. From a document, the **Promote to memory** button in [[Knowledge]] creates a proposal with the passage and its provenance.
