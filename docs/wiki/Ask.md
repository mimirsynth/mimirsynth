# Ask

Here you try **recall**: the same thing an agent does when it queries the memory before answering. It is used to check what your agent would find and to debug why an answer is poor. **Answer** goes one step further and writes the answer, with citations.

## Answer: a written answer with citations
**Mode → Answer** writes a short answer to the question from the same recall, and cites where each claim comes from: `fact 1`, `episode 2`, `doc 1`. **Context** is the mode described below, which shows the recall itself.
- The answer appears as it is written, at about 70 characters a line. Each citation is a small chip inside the sentence that jumps to its card in **Evidence**, below, and gives it the focus; its text shows on hover. From that card, its label opens the source: a fact its entity page, an episode [[Journal]], a document passage its document in [[Knowledge]]. What another brain lends has no link there, only its name.
- Under the answer, **Evidence** lists everything the recall brought, numbered, with what the answer cited highlighted. It is split as in Context: current facts (with when they became true and what they replace), facts that stopped being true (with when), episode fragments and document passages. A fragment or passage that states something no longer true carries «May be superseded» with what is true now, and the model gets the same warning, so it does not present that statement as current.
- The model is told to use **only** that evidence, to say so when it is not enough, and to treat the text of facts, episodes and documents as **data, never instructions**. A citation to something that was not in the evidence is marked «citation with no source» and leads nowhere.
- If the recall finds nothing, it answers that there is no information **without calling the model**.
- It answers in your language, whatever the language of the documents.
- Each answer is one call to the LLM engine chosen under *Engine for «Answer»* (the default one if empty). It counts in the brain's [[AI consumption]] as «Written answers», has a limit per person and hour (*Answers per person and hour*, 30 by default), and waits while the brain's AI budget is used up with pause. See [[Settings reference]].
- The model can still get a summary wrong: the citations are there to check it.

## How to use it (Context)
1. Write a question or a sentence in natural language (e.g. "where is the production database now?").
2. Adjust, if you like, **Facts** (how many facts to bring, 0–30) and **Fragments** (how many episode fragments, 0–15).
3. Press **Search** or `Ctrl+Enter` (`⌘+Enter` on a Mac); in **Answer** mode the same button and keys write the answer. Above the results, a line says which question they answer and what came back, and marks them «From an earlier question» once you change the text.

## What it returns
- **Facts:** the most similar to the question, only above the *Minimum similarity of a fact or a fragment* (which also applies to episode fragments), in two lists as the agent receives them: **Current facts** and **Facts no longer true** (struck through, with their date range). Each shows its similarity. A current fact that replaces one no longer true says «Replaces …», and the one it replaced says «Now: …» and which fragment or passage states it; each name is a link to its card. A fact that joined by that link and not by closeness shows «no similarity». If nothing reaches the threshold, Ask says so. Each card shows its place in the result («no. 1»), the order in which the agent reads them, and names the relation in words («hosted on») with each entity coloured by its type. A fact with no similarity is there because of a link, and says which: it replaces a fact no longer true, or a fragment or passage states it. A fact or fragment whose similarity is within 0.05 of the threshold says «at the edge of the threshold»: a small rise would drop it.
- **Episode fragments:** pieces of the episodes' **original text**, found by meaning and by words. They recover details extraction may have lost. A fragment of an episode that stated something no longer true is marked **May be superseded** above its text, with what stopped being true and what is true now, and goes last.
- **Document passages:** the passages of indexed documents that recall brought, each with its title, when its page was last updated and its similarity. A passage that states something no longer true is marked **May be superseded**, as fragments are, and goes last: the passage of an old decision goes after the one of the update that replaced it. There are at most as many as the brain's *Document passages per recall* (2 by default), only above the *Minimum similarity of a passage*, and the same text found in two documents is shown once (see [[Recall and ingestion]]).
- **Injected context:** the block of text an agent would receive: current facts, the evolution of the facts of the mentioned entities and the cited document passages. On a wide screen it sits beside the cards and stays in view while they scroll, with «Copy» in its header; on a phone it comes last. A link at the top of the results («Go to the injected context») leads to it at any width, so the keyboard reaches it without crossing every card. When the recall brings nothing it says so, and there is nothing to copy.
- **Timings:** total milliseconds and broken down (embedding, facts, fragments).

If another brain lends its memory to this one (federation), the items that come from it carry that brain's name.

## How it is combined
Facts and fragments are ranked separately and then **fused** (reciprocal rank) giving a bit more weight to **recent** items: a question about "now" favours the latest known. No LLM is involved, which is why it is fast (typically tens or a few hundred milliseconds).

## The text index
Fragments come from an **index of the episode text** stored in PostgreSQL. In the header you see its status ("index: N fragments from M episodes"). It synchronises by itself every few minutes (see [[Recall and ingestion]]); **curators** can press **Reindex now** to force it. If it says "disabled", the instance has no PostgreSQL with pgvector and only facts will be searched.

## Saving a reference query (curator or higher)
Curators and above see two tabs, **Ask** and **Reference queries**. After a search, turn on **Mark for a reference query**, under the line that sums up the results, and every fact, fragment and passage gets two buttons: **Expected** (it must come up for this question) and **Extra** (it should not). Turned off, the cards keep only the rating buttons, and what you marked stays marked. **Save as reference query**, next to the switch while it is on, opens the form already filled in with the question and what you marked (the first 200 characters of each result), so the brain can check later that recall still finds it. See [[Reference queries]]. While marking, a marked card has its border in the accent colour, so the list can be scanned for them.

## Rating the results
Under each fact, episode chunk and document passage, **Useful**, **Irrelevant** and **Incorrect** (incorrect only for the brain's own facts) say whether the recall brought the right thing. Anyone who can read the brain can rate, with a limit of requests per minute. The buttons are headed **Rate**, and while marking the other group is headed **Reference query**, so the two are not confused. **Incorrect** stands apart, in red, because on a fact it also proposes a correction: from contributor up it first asks, on the card, **What is wrong?** (what the fact gets wrong and, if you know it, what is true), and **Propose correction** stays off until you write it; the text is the correction's reason, so the curator has something to decide on. Esc or **Cancel** closes it. Under the buttons the card says what the rating did, such as «Rated as incorrect. Its correction was proposed: proposal #26.», with a link to the pending corrections for curators, and a screen reader announces it. Pressing another rating replaces it, but a correction already proposed stays in the queue, and the card says so. With the keyboard, each card's buttons are one stop for Tab: the arrows (and Home/End) move between them, and Tab goes on to the next card.
- **Incorrect** on a fact, from a contributor or above, proposes its **correction** (see [[Proposals]]): a curator decides it, and nothing is invalidated from here. From a reader it is only recorded, and curators see it in **Quality**.
- Every recall, here and over the API and MCP, returns a `recall_id` that can be rated for 48 hours; a recall nobody rated is then forgotten. The recall is logged after it answers, so rating never slows it down; if the log could not be written, that `recall_id` simply cannot be rated. Of the query only a fingerprint and its length are kept, unless the brain chooses to keep its text (see below).

## Quality (curator or higher)
The **Quality** tab sums up the last 30 days: how many recalls there were and how many were rated, and per kind of item (facts, episode chunks, document passages) and per similarity range how much was useful, irrelevant or incorrect. The ranges are what tell whether a threshold such as `doc_min_similarity` should go up or down; **Download CSV** takes them out. Ratings mostly arrive when something fails, so read them next to the total of recalls. It also lists the latest facts rated as incorrect, with their correction.

Brain administrators can tick **Keep the text of the queries**: real queries carry people's prompts, so by default only a fingerprint is kept; with it, the text is kept for 30 days, and unticking it deletes it at once. Ratings follow the brain's retention period for **Recall ratings** ([[Brains and members]]).

**Keep a sample of queries to replay them** is a separate choice, also for brain administrators and off by default. It keeps the last real recalls (1,000 by default, for 30 days): the question with **every** secret the scanner finds masked, whatever the brain's secret mode; the parameters it was asked with; and what it returned (only the kind and id of each item, never its text). They will be replayed against a candidate configuration (a threshold, an embedding model, a release) to see what would change before changing it ([#291](https://github.com/mimirsynth/mimirsynth/issues/291)). Unticking it deletes the sample at once. The limits can be changed through the API (`PUT /api/brains/{slug}/replay/settings` with `enabled`, `max` from 10 to 10,000 and `days` from 1 to 365), and `GET …/replay/samples` lists the last 100 kept.

**Replaying the sample** (brain administrators): with the sample on, **Replay the sample with another configuration** appears below it. Choose a minimum similarity of a fact, the weight by origin, the age by relation or the current state first (each «As now», on or off) and how many queries, and press **Replay**. Each run shows how many queries would change, the items they would gain and lose, the share that would come back empty and the share with something outdated. **See the detail** lists the queries that change, each with **Reference query** to turn it into one. Over the API, `POST /api/brains/{slug}/replay/runs` with a `candidate` asks the newest questions of the sample again. The candidate can set `min_similarity`, `min_sim`, `docs`, `facts`, `chunks`, `trust`, `decay` or `state_card`, and `sample` sets how many questions to ask (100 by default, 500 at most). It changes nothing for callers. `GET …/replay/runs/{id}` gives, per question, the items it would gain and lose and whether the order changes; in all, how many questions change, the share that would come back empty (before and after) and the share that would bring something marked as possibly outdated. Each question is one embedding; there are at most 10 runs per brain and hour, and the last 20 are kept. `POST …/replay/samples/{id}/promote` with `expect` turns a real question into a reference query ([[Reference queries]]), once you confirm what it should bring.

## Scope: this brain or all my brains
**Scope → All my brains** runs the same search at once in **every brain you can read**, with the results grouped by brain: facts, episode fragments and document passages. **This brain** (the default) is the search described above. The browser remembers the last choice; with a single brain there is nothing to choose and the selector is not shown.
- **What is not offered across brains:** the **Answer** mode, the **ratings** and **Save as reference query**. They need one brain's recall (a rating points to a `recall_id`, a reference query belongs to one brain), so with «All my brains» only the context of each brain is shown, with a note saying so.
- **Whose brains:** yours, each with your own role, or all of them if you administer the instance. Federation does not enter: a brain that only lends to another is not searched through it. Brains you cannot read never appear, not even as a count.
- **Each result opens in its brain:** an entity, the episode in Journal or the document in Knowledge, after switching to that brain. **Open in Ask** switches to that brain with the scope back on «This brain» and the question filled in.
- **Fast and robust:** the query's vector is computed once for every brain, which is the costly part. Brains are searched a few at a time, and the page waits at most 8 seconds: a brain that does not answer in time or fails is marked, and the others are shown anyway.
- **Not for API tokens:** they stay bound to one brain, so agents keep asking each brain on its own.
- **Limits:** each person may search all their brains `search_per_hour` times per hour (60 by default, [[Settings reference]]), and the instance runs a few such searches at a time: past either limit it answers «too many» and you can search one brain with «This brain» meanwhile.
- **AI consumption:** the query's vector counts in the brain you have open (or the first one searched), as a recall. If that brain has used up its AI budget with pause, the next one pays; if all of yours have, the search waits for next month. The other brains reuse the vector and cost nothing.

## If the result is poor
| Symptom | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Returns nothing | Empty brain or embeddings down | [[Overview]]; test the engine in [[AI providers]] |
| Brings old facts | Recent episodes missing or not ingested | [[Jobs]], [[Proposals]] |
| Returns the same item under three names | Duplicate entities | [[Content quality]] → Duplicates |
| A concrete detail is missing | It was in the text, not extracted as a fact | Look at the *fragments*; consider rephrasing the episode |
| No document passages, or irrelevant ones | Passage threshold too high or too low for the embedding model | Measure it with [[Reference queries]] (**Simulate**) before changing it |
